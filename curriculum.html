The Curry Protocol

Nine books, 80 weeks, about 15 hours a week. Curry was built on fundamentals at absurd volume, drills graded pass or fail, film review and a coach who wouldn’t let him skip. This is that, for algorithms, systems and math.

How a gate works

Every chapter group ends in a gate. Pass all four checks plus the chapter’s own exit test, or you don’t move on.

G1 Exercises

80% solved unaided. A hint after 30 minutes earns half credit; a seen solution earns none and is redone in 3 days.

G2 Blank page

Reproduce the key algorithm or proof from an empty file, no notes, inside a time cap set beforehand.

G3 Teach-back

300 words for a sharp 17-year-old. No jargon you can’t define. Saved in the repo.

G4 Day-7 retest

Five closed-book questions you wrote on day 0. Pass is 4 of 5.

  • At most 2 gates open. No new chapter until one closes.
  • Fail a check: redo only what failed, retest after 3 days. Three fails: re-teach aloud, restart G1.
  • Every 6 weeks, retest 5 closed gates cold. Below 80%, the gate reopens.
  • Log every attempt in gates.md. Report failures in the next post.

Weekly shape: LeetCode 45 min Mon to Fri. Theory Mon and Thu, math Tue and Fri, 90 min each. Build on Saturday, 3 h. Sunday: 60-min timed scrimmage and 30-min review.

LeetCode ladder

NeetCode 150 as the spine, topped up in your weak patterns. About 250 problems banked, not 1,000. Banked means a clean cold re-solve, with no hints and inside the clock, at day 10. Re-solve at days 3, 10 and 30. Clocks: 15 min Easy, 25 Medium, 40 Hard. Before coding, write the brute force, its complexity and three test cases.

Film: the blog

Every post has a claim, evidence (code, graph or proof), what went wrong, and the next gate with a date. No checkpoint post, no new phase. Post 0 is the pledge: why, the stack, the rules, your honest baseline, the skip list, and how people can hold you to it.

Falling behind? Shift the calendar, never compress it. Three weeks with no gate closed: cut load by a third for two weeks, but keep the daily rep.
